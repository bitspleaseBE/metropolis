# Characters

Canon for the playable cast. The short menu blurbs in the viewer stay as flavour; this page is who they are when the story starts.

They have no crew name. People who know say *"the three."*

---

## Pieter

**Menu:** *Antwerp boy. Jacket, jeans, and the killer smile.*

Flemish. Grew up between Klein Antwerpen and the cafés on Mechelsesteenweg — the kind of face that still gets a nod from the man behind the counter. His uncles and cousins work the docks and the trucks. They like him at family parties. They do not put him on the list when a box needs a reliable cut.

He is blood-adjacent: useful for driving, for smiling at a checkpoint, for knowing which café is listening. He is not blood enough for the room where the money is counted.

**What he brings**

- Access to dock talk, cars, and the Flemish floor without looking like a tourist
- Calm under traffic; he thinks in routes and one-way streets
- A public face the other two can hide behind when they need to look like neighbours

**What he lacks**

- Stones mean nothing to him; the diamond quarter is a foreign country
- On a Borgerhout corner he is the wrong accent and the wrong jacket
- He underestimates how fast a smile stops working once heat rises

**Desire:** a seat at the table his uncles will never offer. If the three build an empire, he wants the port to answer to *them*, not to family charity.

---

## Mo

**Menu:** *Youthful rascal. Grey hoodie, sneakers, and grit.*

Moroccan-Belgian. The Arab Antwerp this faction actually is — Maghrebi streets, Borgerhout corners, cash shops and kebab tills. Turkish Antwerp is a different map; it is not his crew and not his story.

He is young and visible. The older dealers use him for runs and collections because he is fast and he does not flinch. They do not promote him because he is too loud, too seen, too likely to bring police attention into a quiet arrangement.

**What he brings**

- Street literacy: who owns a corner, who is bluffing, who will fold if you stand there long enough
- Access to cash businesses and the soft pressure of *protection*
- Nerve — he will spray a wall or chase a dealer when the others are still talking

**What he lacks**

- The port is sealed to him; a quay badge and a Flemish surname open doors he cannot
- In the diamond quarter he reads as heat, not as a trader
- Patience. He wants respect now; empires are slow

**Desire:** to stop being the boy they send and start being the name they fear on a street. The mark on a wall is his idea of a signature before they had the word for it.

---

## Jacob

**Menu:** *Traditional and kind. Black coat, white shirt, black hat.*

Hassidic. His coat, hat, and beard are the same silhouette the sidewalk already knows. Among his own he is camouflage. Everywhere else he is a flag.

He is trusted with closed parcels and closed mouths. He is not trusted with the stone inside the parcel. The discreet network that moves value through the diamond quarter will use a careful young man as a courier. It will not teach him the cut, the client, or the plane.

**What he brings**

- Passage through the diamond streets without looking like a thief
- Trust as currency — people believe a quiet man with a closed envelope
- A moral weight the other two lean on even when they pretend they do not

**What he lacks**

- Guns and grenades are poison to him; scandal is worse than a lost stone
- On a violent corner his coat makes him a witness people remember
- He still wants to believe some of the elders are only traders

**Desire:** to stop carrying other men’s secrets. If value can leave on a plane, he wants to know *whose* value — and to decide whether the three are building something he can live with.

## How they fit

| | Pieter | Mo | Jacob |
|---|--------|----|-------|
| Floor he came from | Port / Flemish cafés | Street / Borgerhout | Bourse / diamond quarter |
| Public face | Neighbour with a smile | Kid in a hoodie | Traditional and careful |
| Soft power | Routes, cars, dock gossip | Corners, cash, nerve | Trust, stones, silence |
| Soft weakness | Wrong on a corner | Wrong at the quay | Wrong when guns come out |

They stay together because none of them can climb alone, and because the shipment that stranded them made them equally disposable. Loyalty is not romance. It is the only insurance they have bought so far.

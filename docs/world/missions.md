# Missions

Job kinds the three go on. None of this is built yet. These are the shapes of play that turn a walk through Antwerp into a rise.

Each kind is tied to a public pattern from Antwerp’s crime weather — port, stones, cash shops, intimidation, police — without naming real defendants or real shop fronts as targets.

Violence stays inside the trade. Civilians, synagogues, mosques, and ordinary families are not mission objectives.

---

## 1. Spray the mark

**What it is:** Put three short ticks on a wall so a street knows someone new is claiming attention.

**Why it exists:** Walking is anonymous. A tag is the first sentence of an empire that has no name.

**Who fits:** Mo wants it; Pieter watches for witnesses; Jacob hates the attention and still understands the need.

**Linked to reality:** Graffiti and crew marks as territory language — here a fictional mark, not a real gang’s letters.

**Failure:** Wrong wall, wrong witness, heat before you have a corner to stand on.

---

## 2. Chase a dealer off a corner

**What it is:** Stand on someone else’s patch until they leave. Scooters, footsteps, a chase, a message — not a massacre.

**Why it exists:** Corners are the Arab floor’s grammar. The three need one before they can collect or laundry anything.

**Who fits:** Mo leads; Pieter cuts escape routes with local street knowledge; Jacob stays out of the frame.

**Linked to reality:** Street dealing and contested blocks east of the ring, as the news frames them — pressure and displacement, not a shooting gallery.

**Failure:** The dealer comes back with friends, or the police arrive while you are still performing.

---

## 3. Collect protection at a kebab place

**What it is:** Walk into a busy cash business and leave with the week’s understanding paid.

**Why it exists:** Protection is a claim on a street’s rhythm. It funds the three and teaches the map who eats.

**Who fits:** Mo talks; Pieter looks like he could be a customer; Jacob’s coat may help or may terrify — choose the room carefully.

**Linked to reality:** Cash-heavy horeca as a place where informal pressure and dirty money can meet. The shop in the fiction is invented.

**Failure:** The till is empty, the owner has already paid someone older, or you become the loud problem the block reports.

---

## 4. Steal a diamond

**What it is:** Take a stone (or a parcel that should have held one) from the thin criminal edge of the diamond quarter.

**Why it exists:** Diamonds are the Hassidic floor’s native treasure. The three need a win that is not only street cash.

**Who fits:** Jacob gets close; Pieter handles the exit; Mo is the distraction or the threat outside.

**Linked to reality:** Antwerp’s diamond trade as trust, parcels, and enormous value in small packages — fiction at the criminal margin.

**Failure:** The wrong elder remembers your face. A stolen stone can be heavier than a stolen car.

---

## 5. Broker Indians and Jews for a diamond trade

**What it is:** Sit two sides at a table for a deal neither will start without a third party they half-trust.

**Why it exists:** Empires are not only theft. Brokerage is how Jacob’s world makes money without a gun — and how Mo’s pressure outside the room can still matter.

**Who fits:** Jacob in the room; Pieter as clean logistics and a neutral driver; Mo as the reason both sides believe walking away has a cost.

**Linked to reality:** Jewish and Indian traders sharing Antwerp’s diamond sidewalks; most of that trade is legitimate. The mission is the grey deal at the edge.

**Failure:** You look like street muscle in a trust room, or one side uses you and cuts you out.

---

## 6. Send a message at a harbor door

**What it is:** Leave a grenade as a warning at the front door of a fictional harbor worker who touched a box — the city’s underworld dialect for *we know where you sleep*.

**Why it exists:** The port does not respect tags. It respects fear aimed at insiders. This is the ugliest job; it should feel heavy.

**Who fits:** Pieter knows the address world; Mo has the nerve; Jacob should not be there — his coat turns a message into a scandal.

**Linked to reality:** Antwerp crime reporting has long described grenades and door intimidation around the drug trade. The worker and the door are invented. The mission is never aimed at random civilians or religious sites.

**Failure:** Wrong door, collateral panic, or a headline that paints the three as animals instead of a rising firm.

---

## 7. Escape the police on a step

**What it is:** Lose a chase on an e-scooter or similar, using Antwerp’s real street grammar: one-ways, tram beds, premetro mouths, alleys, Velo clutter, dual carriageways.

**Why it exists:** Heat is the tax on every other job. The getaway is how the sandbox’s movement systems become story.

**Who fits:** Whoever drew the short straw; Pieter thinks in routes; Mo thinks in pavements; Jacob is a liability in a coat the cameras love.

**Linked to reality:** Antwerp’s police chases and scooter culture as flavour — the map is the toy.

**Failure:** A dead end that is actually one-way against you, a tram you cannot cross, or a tumble that ends the night.

---

## How jobs should chain

Early jobs teach the mark and the corner. Mid jobs teach cash and stones. Late jobs touch the port’s language and force a choice about how ugly the three are willing to be.

A good night in the fiction ends with: a wall that speaks, a corner that is quieter, a till that is lighter, a stone that moved, a door that understood, and empty asphalt where the blue lights were.

# Trades

The empire the three want is four trades braided together. Each old faction is strong in one braid and poison in another. Profit that lasts has to cross all three of them — which is why boys shut out of every top table can still matter.

This page is roles and pressures. It is not a how-to.

---

## Diamonds

Antwerp’s diamond quarter is a real machine of trust, paper, and stones. Jewish and Indian traders share the sidewalk every day. Most of the business is legitimate. The fiction cares about the thin criminal edge: parcels, grey deals, value that can leave without a siren.

| | Strong | Poison |
|---|--------|--------|
| Hassidic network | Trust, pricing, quiet exit | Scandal, street heat, open guns |
| Indian counterparties | Volume, global clients, hard bargaining | Being forced into a deal by street muscle |
| Flemish dock families | Stealing a bag, moving a courier | Sitting a real stone deal |
| Arab street crews | Scaring a runner | Looking like traders on Pelikaanstraat |
| **The three** | Jacob at the table, Mo as pressure outside, Pieter as clean logistics | Any of them alone |

**Story pressure:** a stone is small, expensive, and social. Steal the wrong one and you have made an enemy who never raises his voice. Broker a deal and you have made two sides who will remember who brought them together.

## Drugs

Cocaine through the port is the headline Antwerp cannot shake: containers, rip-offs, corrupted hands, violence that follows the box inland. Street dealing is the noisy last mile.

| | Strong | Poison |
|---|--------|--------|
| Flemish dock families | Boxes, silence, quay access | Owning a Borgerhout corner long-term |
| Arab street crews | Corners, distribution, collections | Opening a container office |
| Hassidic network | Financing, washing value after the fact | Touching the street product |
| **The three** | Pieter near the docks, Mo on the corner | Jacob holding product in a coat people remember |

**Story pressure:** the port is the source; the corner is the argument. The three start by taking scraps — chasing a dealer, collecting a debt — and aim at controlling the hallway between quay and street without becoming anyone’s errand boys again.

## Money laundering

Dirty euros need a boring face: a busy till, a car invoice, a diamond invoice, a rent that always gets paid. Cash businesses are not automatically criminal. In the fiction, some of them are the soft machine.

| | Strong | Poison |
|---|--------|--------|
| Arab street crews | Horeca and night-shop cash rhythm | Looking clean to a bank for big sums |
| Hassidic network | Paper that looks like trade | A trail that hits the newspapers |
| Flemish dock families | Vehicles, shell logistics, cash in envelopes | Maghrebi street tills they do not control |
| **The three** | Mo’s shops, Jacob’s paper, Pieter’s cars | Greed that makes the till too fat too fast |

**Story pressure:** protection money is not only income — it is a claim that a street’s cash belongs in *your* rhythm. Laundry is how a corner becomes an empire instead of a weekend.

## Weapons

Guns and grenades sit under Antwerp’s crime news as tools of intimidation and as cargo. A warning at a front door — including a grenade left as a message — is a known language in this city’s underworld reporting. The fiction uses that language against invented insiders, never against random civilians or houses of worship.

| | Strong | Poison |
|---|--------|--------|
| Flemish dock families | Supply, storage, door messages | Using that language near the bourse |
| Arab street crews | Street use, fear on a block | A full port pipeline |
| Hassidic network | Paying for distance from the problem | Any scandal that puts a coat next to a grenade |
| **The three** | Pieter’s access, Mo’s nerve for delivery of a message | Jacob’s presence at the scene |

**Story pressure:** weapons are not the win condition. They are the punctuation. Overuse turns the three into the kind of loud problem the old men erase.

## How a profit has to move

A complete loop looks like this in the fiction:

1. Something valuable arrives or is freed at the **port** (Flemish floor).
2. It becomes street control or street cash on a **corner** (Arab floor).
3. Surplus becomes quiet value through **laundry** and sometimes a **stone** (Hassidic floor / Indian table).
4. **Weapons** keep the loop from being casually stolen — until the weapons become the story, and then everyone loses.

The old factions fight to own the whole loop. They fail because each is allergic to someone else’s floor. The three survive by being the allergy medicine: one boy per floor, one mark, no public name.

# Streets

Street rules for activities that are still only ideas. When mechanics get built later, they should obey this page so the city stays a crime drama and does not slide into racism or random violence.

This is not a build plan. It is how the fiction behaves on the pavement.

---

## The mark

Three short ticks, side by side, about the height of a hand.

- It means *the three were here* and *this street is in play*.
- It is not a brand, a hashtag, or a spoken crew name.
- Spraying it on a house of worship, a school, or a random family’s door is outside the fiction. Walls that already take tags — side streets, shutters, underpasses — are the grammar.
- A mark without a follow-up (corner, collection, deal) is just paint. The old factions laugh; then they paint over it.

---

## Corners

A corner is a claim on who sells and who watches.

- Clearing a dealer is about presence, chase, and humiliation of a rival *in the trade* — not executing pedestrians.
- Winning a corner raises your rhythm on that block: collections get easier, tags stick, heat may rise.
- Losing a corner means your mark looks like a lie. Paint over your own ticks or watch someone else do it.
- Pieter is weak here; Mo is strong; Jacob should not be the face of the clear.

---

## Protection

Protection is a weekly understanding with a cash business that already lives on cash.

- You are buying calm and claiming the till’s surplus rhythm — not playing a hate crime against an ethnicity.
- The shop in the story is fictional. Real kebab places and night shops in Antwerp are not hit lists.
- Refusal, empty tills, and double-payers (already paying an older crew) are part of the drama.
- Beating the staff for sport is outside the ethic. Pressure can be ugly; it stays instrumental.

---

## Stones and rooms

The diamond quarter runs on trust faces and closed doors.

- Theft missions target the criminal margin: parcels, grey couriers, invented elders — not tourists, not synagogues.
- Brokerage missions put Jacob in the room and keep street muscle *outside* unless the deal requires a visible cost to walking away.
- Indian traders are counterparties. Treat them as sharp professionals, not as a cartoon faction.

---

## Harbor messages

A grenade at a door is a sentence in Antwerp’s underworld dialect: *we know where you sleep.*

- The target is a fictional insider who touched a box — worker, middleman, or someone who talked.
- The sentence is never aimed at random civilians, children, or religious buildings.
- Success is fear and silence, not a body count. If the fiction needs an explosion, the weight should land on consequence and heat, not spectacle for its own sake.
- Jacob’s presence at that door is a writing mistake unless the point is scandal.

---

## Heat

Heat is the city’s immune system: police attention, faction notice, cameras, neighbours who call.

- Every loud job raises it. Tags less than grenades; grenades more than collections.
- Heat changes how the map feels: more blue lights, shorter patience on corners, closed doors at the bourse.
- A police unit that sees you run a red light, or hurt someone, puts sheriff stars on you. Up to four. The same combi and the same two officers, duplicated. Stay out of their sight and the stars fall, one at a time.
- Killing an officer or a crew member adds a star. A clan sign on that meter is not something you can trigger. A mission calls it, and then a black car with four armed members flies that clan's drawing. Same chase, same cap of four.
- Escaping on a step is how heat gets spent down — if you know the one-ways and the tram beds.
- Killing random pedestrians for cash (today’s sandbox pickup) does not belong to this heat model. It is not how the three build.

---

## The step

E-scooters and light wheels are the getaway dialect of a dense, one-way city.

- The toy is the real street grammar already in the viewer: dual carriageways, tram tracks, premetro, alleys, Velo clutter.
- A good escape feels like Antwerp: illegal turns that cars cannot take, pavements that bikes can, a chase that dies at a gate or a crowd.
- Crashing into civilians as a strategy is outside the ethic. Collisions can happen; hunting people with a scooter cannot be the win condition.

---

## What never becomes a mission

- Targeting people for being Flemish, Arab, Jewish, or Indian
- Attacks on synagogues, mosques, schools, or family homes as entertainment
- Torture or hate speech as progression
- A “race war” meter or anything that scores cruelty against a community

Factions are criminal currents. The city around them is full of people who share a language or a coat with a faction and still have nothing to do with the trade. The three know the difference. The docs insist on it so the game can too.

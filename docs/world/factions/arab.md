# Faction: Arab (street crews)

A criminal current in Maghrebi Antwerp — not Arab Antwerp itself. Most Moroccan-Belgian and broader Maghrebi families in the city are neighbours, shopkeepers, students, and parents. This page is about the crews that treat corners and cash tills as territory.

Mo is from this world. He is young enough to be used and visible enough to be denied a promotion.

Turkish Antwerp is a different map: different cafés, different uncles, different rivalries. It can appear as weather on a street. It is not this faction’s roster.

## Who they are

Street crews and the older men who sit behind them: corner dealers, collectors, owners of busy shops that take cash when questions get expensive. They think in blocks, scooters, who is watching, and how long the police stay interested.

They prefer problems that look like respect. A wall that speaks. A dealer who finds another corner. A shop that pays so the week stays quiet.

## Strengths

- **Corners.** Who sells where, who bluffs, who folds. Clearing a dealer is their grammar.
- **Cash and laundry.** Kebab places, night shops, busy horeca — tills that already live on cash are the soft machine that turns street money into rent and invoices.
- **Speed and visibility.** Scooters, steps, kids who know every alley. When something needs to happen tonight, this floor moves first.
- **Protection as rhythm.** Not random violence: a weekly understanding that a business on their street pays for calm. Ugly when it is; still a trade, not a pogrom.

## Weaknesses

- **The port.** Quay access, union-adjacent silence, and Flemish dock families are a wall. A hoodie does not open a container office.
- **The diamond quarter.** Inside Pelikaanstraat they read as heat. They cannot sit a Jewish–Indian stone deal; they can only scare a courier and make everything worse.
- **Quiet scandal.** Loud beef brings police. The older men hate Mo’s generation for exactly that reason — and still use them.
- **Weapons at scale.** A gun on a corner is common enough; a stocked pipeline from the docks is not theirs unless the Flemish floor is selling.

## Relationship to the three

Mo is their mistake and their future. They will call him a rascal until his mark is on a wall they thought they owned. Then he is competition.

Pieter is a useful outsider for a drive across the ring — never trusted with the till. Jacob in a black coat on their street is a story people will tell the police; they want him gone before a deal, not in the middle of one.

## Territory in the fiction

Borgerhout and the eastern streets; cash shops and kebab fronts wherever the map allows; the soft edge of Klein Antwerpen where a tag can announce that the three are not only an eastern problem.

## Their sign

`viewer/signs/clan-arab.svg` — a crescent. One glyph, the street crews only.

## If you betray them

A mission can call them. They send the same black sedan and four armed members, and the meter shows the crescent. Later they may come on scooters. The crescent stays. Maghrebi neighbours, shops, and mosques are not this chase.
